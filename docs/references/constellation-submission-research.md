## 1. Which Constellation Is This?

**Constellation Institute** — the Berkeley AI safety nonprofit at **constellation.org**. Not an Anthropic program.

- Main site: https://constellation.org
- Astra Fellowship page: https://constellation.org/programs/astra-fellowship
- Application form (Fillout): https://constellation.fillout.com/t/kwFFQj7S2Xus (from prior cycle; current May 3 cohort likely same system)

**Conclusion:** The exact wording is not publicly available. The field is optional and functions as a "show your work" link, not an eligibility gate.

## 3. Bar for a "Serious" Submission

**Constellation explicitly does not require academic publications.** Their own description:
> "Prior AI safety experience is not required. Many of our most impactful fellows entered from adjacent fields and quickly made significant contributions."

The fellowship explicitly targets: technical research, **security**, governance, policy, strategy, field-building — security is called out by name.

**What accepted fellows actually look like:**
- Accepted fellows include people from cognitive science, government, game design (a documented Astra fellow with zero papers), and policy — not just ML PhD students.
- The program's own staff (Henry Sleight, Technical Program Manager) lists a Google Scholar profile with interpretability/safety co-authored papers — but that's a staff member running the program, not a typical applicant bar.
- No evidence that a Google Scholar profile is required or that its absence hurts non-technical-track applicants.

**What the field probably values:**
- For empirical research track: Papers, preprints, arXiv posts, or technical blog posts demonstrating research ability.
- For security track (your case): A GitHub writeup, a public bug report, a contest write-up, a technical blog post, or a preprint is all credible evidence. A Guardian Defender contest report or a detailed write-up of multi-agent orchestration for smart contract auditing is legitimate "research output" — it demonstrates technical depth, original methodology, and real-world security impact.

**The bar is not peer review. It is: "Can you demonstrate you can do rigorous technical work?"**

## 4. Concrete Paths Given Days-to-Weeks Timeline

### Option A: Submit Nothing (Recommended Default)
Leave the field blank. The field is optional. A blank field does not hurt a security researcher applicant on the security/technical track. Your actual work artifacts (GitHub repos, contest results, write-ups) are better presented in the CV and free-text sections than forced into a Scholar URL.

### Option B: SSRN Preprint (~3–5 business days to live)
- SSRN accepts independent researchers with no institutional affiliation. No endorsement required.
- Upload process: create account → submit PDF → SSRN review: **most submissions approved within 3 business days**.
- Google Scholar indexing after SSRN goes live: **1 day to 3 weeks**, typically within 1–2 weeks. Not guaranteed before the May 3 deadline if you start now, but SSRN link itself is immediately shareable even before Scholar picks it up.
- Computer science papers go in SSRN's Computer Science Research Network. Relevant category: AI, security, software engineering.
- **Verdict:** Realistic if you have a write-up ready. Upload this week, get SSRN live in <1 week, Scholar may or may not index in time — but the SSRN URL is submittable regardless.

### Option C: arXiv Preprint (~days to weeks, with friction)
- **New arXiv endorsement policy (January 21, 2026):** First-time submitters now need BOTH an institutional email AND prior co-authorship on an arXiv paper — OR must get personal endorsement from an established arXiv author in the same category.
- As an independent researcher with no institutional affiliation and no prior arXiv papers, you must obtain personal endorsement. This requires finding someone in cs.CR, cs.SE, or cs.AI who has endorsement privileges and is willing to vouch for your paper. Cold requests to strangers take days to weeks and frequently fail.
- Even with endorsement, moderation for first-time submitters in cs.CR can take additional days.
- **Verdict:** Possible but has a real friction point. If you have a connection in the field who can endorse you quickly, this is a ~1 week path. Without a connection, it could take too long or fail entirely before May 3.

### Option D: Google Scholar Manual Profile Entry
- You can create a Google Scholar profile and manually add publications to it — including items that are not indexed (blog posts, technical reports, GitHub documents, contest write-ups).
- You provide the title, authors, year, and an optional URL. The entry appears on your profile immediately.
- This is legitimate and is how independent researchers and practitioners present non-academic work to academic audiences.
- **Verdict:** Immediate. A Scholar profile linking to your multi-agent auditing work, Guardian Defender write-ups, and any public technical documents is perfectly valid and can be created today. It will not have citation counts, but that is expected for non-academic work.

### Option E: Post a Detailed Technical Write-Up
- Publish a substantive technical post (e.g., on a personal site, substack, or as a GitHub repo README) documenting the multi-agent orchestration architecture for smart contract auditing — methodology, novel findings, results.
- Link to this in the application directly, or submit it via Google Scholar manual entry.
- **Verdict:** This is probably the highest-value path for your profile. A 3,000-word technical write-up with diagrams, methodology, and results is more compelling than an SSRN preprint of equivalent content, because it shows communication ability and is directly readable by reviewers.

## 5. Red Flags and Surprises

1. **The current cohort deadline is May 3, 2026 — but the application form shown on the main page is for the September 2026 start cohort, not a Summer 2026 cohort.** Applications for the January 2026 cohort are closed. Confirm you are applying to the right cohort window. The current open application (May 3 deadline) is for the next cohort starting September 2026.

2. **arXiv endorsement tightened significantly in January 2026.** Institutional email alone no longer suffices. Independent researchers without prior arXiv papers now face a real barrier. Do not assume arXiv is a quick path.

3. **SSRN Google Scholar indexing is unpredictable.** SSRN itself says it has no control over when Google crawls new papers. Historical community data suggests 1 day to 3 weeks. With a May 3 deadline, uploading to SSRN now gives reasonable but not guaranteed Scholar indexing in time.

4. **The Constellation fellowship does not appear to be academically gatekeeping.** The "security" focus area is explicitly listed as a target background. A practitioner with demonstrable technical depth (multi-agent AI systems, smart contract auditing, novel tooling) fits the program's stated profile. The Scholar field almost certainly exists to help reviewers find work context quickly, not to filter out non-academics.

5. **No public applicant write-ups document the Google Scholar field specifically.** Every detailed Constellation post found was about the 2023–2024 cohorts, when the field may not have existed or was less prominent. The current application form is on Fillout (gated). No one has published a walkthrough of the current application questions.

## Summary Recommendation

For a security researcher / AI-agent engineer with no traditional publications:

1. **Create a Google Scholar profile today** with manual entries for your most substantive technical outputs (Guardian Defender write-ups, architecture docs, any public technical posts).
2. **Write a clean technical summary** of the multi-agent auditing work if one doesn't exist publicly. This is the most defensible "research artifact" you have. Post it publicly and link it.
3. **Consider SSRN** if you want something more "formal" — upload this week for a reasonable chance of Scholar indexing before May 3.
4. **Skip arXiv** unless you have an existing contact who can endorse you within 2–3 days.
5. **Do not let the Scholar field be a blocker.** The program explicitly welcomes adjacent-field applicants. A blank field with a strong CV and proposal is better than a rushed, thin preprint.
